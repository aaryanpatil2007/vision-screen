research prototype

Comprehensive vision screening

A guided battery of thirteen tests: visual acuity (both eyes and each eye alone), contrast sensitivity, astigmatism, color vision, the central visual field, depth perception, binocular fusion, eye tracking and alignment, pupil response, and a photorefraction estimate of focus. It takes about 15 minutes and runs entirely on this machine — your video is analyzed locally and never leaves it.

A screening tool, not an eye exam. It cannot measure eye pressure, see your retina, or rule out disease. In one study of adults who considered themselves healthy (median age 70), about one in three had a finding at a full eye exam that a test like this cannot detect. Use it to decide whether to book an exam — never to skip one. It issues no prescription and makes no diagnosis.

Two tests need red-cyan 3-D glasses (red lens over your right eye). Without them, depth perception and binocular fusion will be skipped — everything else still works.

2 · Calibrate your screen

Hold a credit card (or any ID card) against the screen and drag until the outline matches its width exactly. This converts letter sizes to real angles — without it, acuity numbers are meaningless.

Scale: 37.8 px/cm (card is 8.56 cm wide)

3 · Viewing distance

Measure roughly how far your eyes are from the screen and set it here. Keep this distance for the whole test.

50 cm

4 · Screen brightness

Turn your screen brightness up to at least 75%. Acuity standards specify 80–320 cd/m²; at a laptop's minimum brightness people measure about 1.3 lines worse than they really are, which is enough to invent a problem that isn't there. The browser cannot read your brightness, so this one is on you.

The bar below should look like a smooth gradient with every step distinguishable. If the dark end is a single black block, raise brightness.

5 · Before you start

• Wear your usual glasses or contacts only if you want your corrected vision tested — otherwise remove them.

• Set screen brightness to maximum for the contrast and color tests.

• You'll be asked to cover one eye at a time — use your palm, not your fingers.

• The last two tests need a dark room. You can dim the lights when prompted.
