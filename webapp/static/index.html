A screening tool, not an eye exam. It cannot measure eye pressure, see your retina, or rule out disease. In one study of adults who considered themselves healthy, about one in three had a finding at a full exam that a test like this cannot detect. Use it to decide whether to book an exam — never to skip one. It issues no prescription and makes no diagnosis.

Step two

Calibrate the screen

Hold a bank card against the display and drag until the outline matches its width exactly. Every letter size in this test is an angle, and an angle needs to know how big your pixels really are — without this, acuity is meaningless.

Scale 37.8 px/cm  ·  card is 8.56 cm wide (ISO/IEC 7810)

Step three

Viewing distance

Roughly how far are your eyes from the screen? Keep it for the whole test — the camera tracks it and corrects your result if you drift.

50 cm

Step four

About you

Age changes what a result means more than anything else here. The same slightly-reduced contrast is unremarkable at twenty-five and worth checking at seventy-five. Without it, results are read against a forty-five-year-old.

Are you wearing glasses or contacts right now?

This changes what the results mean, not just how precise they are. Without correction the test estimates your focusing error. With it, the test measures how well your current prescription is working — so anything it finds is what your lenses are not already fixing.

Anything you have noticed? Some things cannot be measured through a camera at all — these are the ones only you can report.

Flashes, a burst of new floaters, or a shadow moving across your vision together are an emergency — those need same-day attention, not a screening test.

Step five

Can you darken the room?

Three of the guided tests — pupil response, refraction, and the alignment part of eye tracking — need the screen to be the brightest thing in the room. They read light reflected off your own eye, and daylight drowns it out. The other ten work in any lighting.

Step six

Screen brightness

Turn brightness to at least 75%. Acuity standards call for 80–320 cd/m²; at a laptop's dimmest setting people measure about 1.3 lines worse than they really are — enough to invent a problem that isn't there. The browser cannot read your brightness, so this one is on you.

Every step below should be distinguishable. If the dark end is one black block, raise the brightness.

Step seven

Before you start

Keep your glasses or contacts on. Screening measures the vision you actually live with, and that is what a clinic records too. Removing them measures a refraction this test cannot do properly — and would leave many people unable to see the instructions.

Two tests need red-cyan 3-D glasses, red lens over the right eye. Without them, depth and fusion are skipped.

You'll cover one eye at a time — use your palm, not your fingers.

The last two tests need a dark room. You can dim the lights when prompted.

Research prototype — not FDA-cleared and not a medical device. It issues no prescription and makes no diagnosis. Results describe visual function measured on this screen at this moment, and are not a substitute for a comprehensive eye examination by a licensed professional.
